## Sprint 1 — trustworthy capture foundation

1. **AMA-101: Capture state machine** — **Done in Sprint 1.1.** Idle → preparing → recording → stopping → completed/failed, with guarded transitions and tests. The UI currently drives a clearly labelled no-media simulation.
2. **AMA-102: Select sources** — **Done in Sprint 1.2.** Enumerate active displays, Windows render endpoints and microphone endpoints; identify Windows defaults, refresh on demand and preserve a still-available selection. Persisting choices across app restarts is deferred until settings storage exists.
3. **AMA-103: System audio** — **Done through Sprint 1.4.2.** Record the selected render endpoint and microphone simultaneously into independent `system_audio_*.wav` and `microphone_*.wav` files with a shared timestamp, separate live levels, one elapsed timer, atomic start rollback and exactly-once cleanup. Silent loopback gaps are filled to preserve wall-clock duration. Manual validation measured 13.471 s microphone and 13.613 s system audio (142 ms difference); sample-accurate synchronization remains Sprint 1.6 scope.
4. **AMA-104: Microphone** — **Done in Sprint 1.3.** Record the selected input independently via WASAPI `IAudioClient`, display a live RMS level meter and save finalized PCM16 WAV files under `artifacts/captures/`. Automated validation covers state transitions, format conversion, WAV production, fault/stop races and idempotent cleanup (20/20 Core tests plus 3/3 Windows smoke checks). Manual hardware validation passed with an Intel microphone array: 48 kHz, 16-bit, four-channel WAV, 8.35 seconds, playable output.
5. **AMA-105: Screen/window** — **Sprint 1.5.1 and 1.5.2 complete.** Record the selected display as a local H.264 MP4. The Windows adapter owns the native Media Foundation encoder behind `IScreenCaptureProvider`; oversized sources are proportionally downscaled to encoder-safe dimensions. Manual screen-only validation passed for both displays, including the 5160×2160 source at 3840×1608 output. Sprint 1.5.2 starts screen, system audio and microphone as one atomic lifecycle with a shared artifact timestamp and restores both live audio meters. Manual combined capture and binary artifact validation passed: video 16.633 s, system audio 16.559 s and microphone 16.290 s. The measured 74 ms/343 ms offsets become Sprint 1.6 alignment input; preview/window capture remain later slices.
6. **AMA-106: Session workspace** — **Sprint 1.6.1 through 1.6.3 complete.** Each recording creates a unique `session_*` directory containing all three media artifacts and an atomically replaced, versioned `manifest.json`. A shared monotonic clock and native media parsers produce an alignment classification. At startup, stale `preparing`/`recording` manifests are atomically marked `interrupted` without deleting artifacts; missing, empty and corrupt artifacts remain explicit diagnostics. A real forced-process-termination test recovered one session as `interrupted`, preserved its MP4 and microphone WAV and exposed the zero-byte system-audio WAV. Capture start requires at least 2 GiB free on the target drive. Durable promotion remains later work.
7. **AMA-107: Controls and consent** — Wire start/stop, elapsed time, clear status, consent and failure recovery into the shell. The right-hand card is now correctly labelled **Release roadmap** rather than Processing pipeline. **UI TODO:** redesign the record/stop control; the growing stack of button label, timer and two meters is functional but visually awkward.
8. **AMA-108: Smoke-test matrix** — **Sprint 1.4.3 and Sprint 1.5.3 complete.** Automated coverage includes rapid dual and combined start/stop, simultaneous stream faults, partial start rollback, stop failure, repeated shutdown, silent-loopback timeline preservation, runtime display failure, encoder-safe ultrawide sizing and actionable device/encoder errors. Cleanup of one stream continues even when another stream's stop fails. Manual validation passed combined rapid cycles, close during recording, playable non-empty final artifacts and a successful new recording after restart. Physical USB/display unplug, Teams, Bluetooth switching, sleep and long recordings remain broader matrix items.
9. **AMA-109: Live microphone handover** — Allow changing the microphone during an active meeting when a headset battery dies or the user moves to another device. Finalize the old microphone segment, start a new segment at a recorded session-clock offset, keep system audio running and expose the handover in the future session manifest. Do not append incompatible device formats into one WAV file.
10. **AMA-110: Live screen handover** — Allow switching the selected display during an active meeting without exposing file-management details in the UI. Prefer safe dynamic source reconfiguration; if dimensions or encoder constraints require it, store internal timestamped segments and present them as one meeting recording.

## Sprint 3.5 — Intel GPU acceleration evaluation

- **Sprint 3.5.1 complete: Backend spike and benchmark.** The production WhisperX/CTranslate2 path remains unchanged. An isolated OpenVINO 2026.3/Optimum Intel harness detects CPU, Intel iGPU and NPU, downloads separate OpenVINO weights and compares CPU/GPU compile time, inference time, real-time factor, text, timestamps and footprint. The initial monolithic 39:54 test was manually aborted after 29 minutes and exposed non-linear long-form behavior. The corrected path uses overlapping 30-second blocks, visible progress, bounded timestamps and atomic partial reports. A 199.8-second system-audio sample ran in 47.74 seconds on CPU versus 24.48 seconds on Intel GPU (48.7% faster inference) with near-identical text. The full 39:54 GPU test completed in 13:41 (RTF 0.343), but failed quality acceptance with 129 adjacent exact duplicates, severe repeated-phrase hallucinations and 52,418 output characters versus 16,503 from the validated WhisperX system-audio result. Runtime/model footprint is approximately 252 MB/1.56 GB.
- **Sprint 3.5.2 complete: Native OpenVINO GenAI integration.** Settings expose an explicit `Intel GPU (OpenVINO · Whisper Small)` profile only when the isolated runtime and dedicated FP16 model are locally present. The supervised job retains dual-track normalization, cancellation, atomic output, source merging, speaker diarization and versioned transcript runs. CPU Pyannote VAD gates each track before native Intel-iGPU inference; progress distinguishes model compilation, speech detection, per-source GPU decoding and diarization. Boundary reconciliation sorts and clamps overlapping OpenVINO segments. A two-minute UI run completed in 73.54 seconds as `gpu/openvino-fp16`, preserving six microphone and one system-audio segment plus one detected remote speaker. Manual cancellation during processing and application shutdown both completed without a stranded worker. The rejected Optimum/Transformers adapter and Intel NPU remain research-only.

## Sprint 3.6 — incremental processing during capture

- **Sprint 3.6.1: Capture-safe rolling chunks — Implemented, manual smoke pending.** Fork immutable PCM16 buffers from both audio tracks into bounded, non-blocking background queues. Finalize atomic 30-second WAV chunks plus a per-track manifest under `processing/live-chunks/`. Queue pressure may degrade only the live preparation and is represented by inserted silence and diagnostics; it must never block or fail the primary recording.
- **Sprint 3.6.2: Incremental speech preparation — Complete.** Finalized microphone/system-audio chunks enter one bounded FIFO and are transcribed sequentially at below-normal process priority while capture continues. Per-chunk schema-3 results are stored under `processing/live-transcripts/<source>/`; explicit source labels survive generic chunk filenames. Queue pressure and worker failures are warnings only: durable chunks remain available for Sprint 3.6.3 catch-up and neither capture nor Stop may fail. Manual hardware validation confirmed stable meters/timer, responsive Stop, playable capture masters and valid source-labelled live transcript artifacts while inference was active.
- **Sprint 3.6.3: Final reconciliation — Complete.** After Stop, drain active live jobs and retry every missing durable chunk. Shift chunk-local segments onto the meeting timeline, clamp them to chunk bounds and trim repeated word sequences at adjacent boundaries. Persist the merged evidence, then run one optimized CPU/XPU speaker analysis over the full system-audio master without re-running Whisper over the meeting. Publish an immutable versioned schema-3 transcript plus the canonical latest pointer; cancellation/failure preserves capture masters, chunks and partial results for retry. The 46-second hardware smoke exposed and fixed PascalCase/camelCase duplication at the C#/Python boundary. Reprocessing the preserved evidence produced 16 valid segments across microphone/system audio, one remote speaker and a 47.49-second final timeline. Cold XPU/Pyannote finalization took about 153 seconds versus about 37 seconds warm; persistent model warming is a dedicated optimization TODO.
- **Later optional speaker identification.** Once stable speaker turns exist, offer a dismissible/opt-in prompt to name detected speakers while the meeting continues. Never interrupt capture, steal focus repeatedly or require names before finalization; remember the user's dismissal for that meeting.

## Sprint 3.8 — processing optimization

- **Sprint 3.8.1:** Pair contemporaneous microphone and system-audio chunks into one isolated transcription job. Both tracks share one model load while remaining separate inputs, languages and source-labelled output artifacts. A short 750 ms collection window keeps capture non-blocking and moves inference startup away from the microphone handover interaction. Short meetings below 90 seconds skip full Pyannote speaker analysis automatically; silent system audio already skips it at the speech-duration gate.
- **Sprint 3.8.2 complete:** Feed final Pyannote only conservative ASR-derived system-speech windows instead of the complete loopback timeline. Preserve context with padding and gap merging, restore turns to original timestamps and persist honest finalization/analyzed-duration metrics. On the same 46:34 reference meeting, 31 windows reduced analyzed audio from 2,791.374 to 1,712.212 seconds. Finalization fell from about 9:34 to 6:27 (32.5% faster), while all 628 text/timeline segments and seven speaker IDs remained. Assigned system segments changed from 392 to 389, unassigned from 16 to 18 and ambiguous from one to two: a measured 0.73% assignment trade-off concentrated in short boundary phrases.
- **Sprint 3.8.3 complete, candidate rejected:** A bounded benchmark-only XPU profile override compared segmentation step 0.20 against 0.15 on identical 3.8.2 evidence. Runtime improved only from 6:27 to 6:13, while speaker clusters fell from seven to five by merging three 30.1/7.2/3.0-second fragments. Assignment metrics improved slightly, but participant count alone cannot prove those voices belong together. False splits remain repairable by assigning the same display name to multiple technical IDs; false merges destroy separability. The 15-second gain does not justify that risk, so conservative step 0.15 remains production default and 0.20 remains benchmark-only.
- **Sprint 3.8.4 complete:** After a speaker-enriched transcript is atomically published, remove only reproducible high-volume processing data: rolling WAV chunks, paired-job scratch space, temporary normalized WAVs and the preliminary copy. Retain capture masters, canonical/versioned final transcripts, merged incremental evidence and per-source chunk transcript JSON. Cleanup is session-path constrained and best-effort; warnings never invalidate a successful transcript, while cancellation or finalizer failure performs no cleanup. Manual hardware validation confirmed the successful cleanup path.
- **Sprint 3.8.5 complete:** Publish the reconciled source-labelled transcript immediately after catch-up under a protected preliminary path and atomically promote it to the canonical transcript pointer. The UI and Meeting Library can open it while speaker enrichment continues in the background. Successful enrichment atomically replaces the pointer; cancellation/failure leaves the usable preliminary transcript available. Manual validation confirmed both preliminary viewing and final replacement.
- **Next measurements:** Compare cold/warm post-processing and model-load counts on a two-minute meeting. Consider a supervised persistent OpenVINO runner only if paired jobs still spend unacceptable time loading models; cancellation and crash isolation remain acceptance gates.
- **Sprint 3.9 planned:** Capture Health Monitoring will detect missing/never-seen microphone or system audio, warn during capture, suggest active alternative endpoints, validate final audio evidence and avoid pointless transcription of empty streams.

## Sprint 3.9 — capture health and mute semantics

- **Sprint 3.9.1 complete:** Observe real RMS evidence independently for selected system audio and microphone without touching the capture path. - **Sprint 3.9.3 complete:** Poll Core Audio endpoint meters only after the selected source becomes unhealthy.
